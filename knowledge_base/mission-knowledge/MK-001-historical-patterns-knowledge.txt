SECTION 1 — KNOWN FAILURE PATTERNS
================================================================================

1.1 THERMAL CASCADE PATTERN
-----------------------------
Description:
  A thermal cascade occurs when battery temperature elevation triggers a
  chain reaction affecting multiple spacecraft systems. AETHER-1 has
  experienced 3 confirmed thermal cascade events.

Pattern Signature:
  Battery Temperature rising > 55°C
  → Solar Panel Output elevated or Attitude deviation present
  → CPU Temperature beginning to rise (delayed 8-15 minutes)
  → Signal Strength degrading if attitude affected

Historical Occurrences:
  Mission Day 47  — IR-001 (CRITICAL — full cascade)
  Mission Day 89  — IR-007 (WARNING — partial cascade, contained early)
  Mission Day 156 — IR-005 (WARNING — CPU/attitude cascade, no thermal component)

Early Warning Indicators:
  - Battery temperature rising >2°C per 10-minute window
  - Concurrent attitude deviation beyond ±1.5 degrees
  - Solar panel output above 210W during expected nominal period

Recommended Response:
  On detection of thermal cascade signature — immediately execute:
  1. Power load reduction
  2. Attitude assessment and correction
  3. CPU load reduction
  Do not wait for CRITICAL threshold. Early intervention contains cascade.

1.2 COMMUNICATION DEGRADATION PATTERN
----------------------------------------
Description:
  Signal degradation events on AETHER-1 follow a predictable multi-stage
  pattern. Recognizing early stages allows intervention before complete loss.

Pattern Signature:
  Stage 1 — Signal strength declining trend over 10-minute window
  Stage 2 — Data transmission rate dropping below 80 kbps
  Stage 3 — Signal strength crossing -85 dBm
  Stage 4 — Complete signal loss

Historical Occurrences:
  Mission Day 66  — IR-002 (CRITICAL — complete blackout 47 minutes)
  Mission Day 95  — IR-009 (WARNING — partial degradation, solar storm related)
  Mission Day 141 — Minor degradation event, resolved via attitude adjustment

Key Finding:
  In 100% of AETHER-1 communication anomalies, attitude stability deviation
  was either a primary cause or contributing factor. Monitoring attitude
  stability is the best leading indicator for communication health.

Recommended Response:
  At Stage 2 — immediately check attitude stability and execute correction.
  At Stage 3 — switch to X-band backup without waiting for complete loss.
  Early frequency switching reduced average recovery time by 67% in testing.

1.3 POWER BUDGET STRESS PATTERN
----------------------------------
Description:
  Power budget stress occurs when the combination of reduced solar generation
  and elevated system consumption creates an unsustainable power deficit.

Pattern Signature:
  Solar Panel Output declining (below 190W)
  + Battery Charge Level declining simultaneously
  + High CPU utilization or elevated temperatures

Historical Occurrences:
  Mission Day 82  — IR-006 (extended eclipse — battery depletion to 38%)
  Mission Day 133 — IR-004 (panel degradation — 13-day gradual decline)

Key Finding:
  Battery charge level alone is a lagging indicator. Solar panel output
  trend is the leading indicator. When output drops below 195W and shows
  a declining trend, power budget stress intervention should begin
  immediately — before battery charge level begins to decline.

Recommended Response:
  1. Implement power load shedding at first declining trend detection
  2. Adjust attitude for maximum solar exposure
  3. Defer non-essential high-power operations

1.4 PROPULSION SYSTEM DEGRADATION PATTERN
-------------------------------------------
Description:
  Fuel pressure anomalies on AETHER-1 have consistently followed a slow
  degradation curve rather than sudden drops, indicating filter or line issues
  rather than catastrophic leaks.

Pattern Signature:
  Fuel pressure declining at rate of 5-15 kPa per hour
  No corresponding velocity change in short term
  No thruster activation logged

Historical Occurrences:
  Mission Day 113 — IR-003 (filter occlusion — gradual 3-hour decline)
  Mission Day 91  — IR-008 (thruster performance degradation — separate pattern)

Key Finding:
  Gradual pressure decline without velocity impact indicates a supply-side
  restriction (filter/line). Sudden pressure drop with velocity anomaly
  indicates thruster or valve issue. These require different responses.

Recommended Response for Gradual Decline:
  Execute fuel line purge procedure FP-003 as first intervention.
  Success rate: 87% based on mission history.

SECTION 2 — SYSTEM INTERDEPENDENCY MAP
================================================================================

The following interdependencies have been validated through AETHER-1 mission
experience. These are not theoretical — each has been observed in-mission.

  CPU Temperature → Attitude Stability
    High CPU load causes thermal throttling which delays attitude control
    loop execution, directly degrading stability. (Confirmed: IR-005)

  Attitude Stability → Signal Strength
    Attitude deviation misaligns directional antenna, causing signal loss.
    Deviation of ±3.5 degrees typically causes signal loss within 8 minutes.
    (Confirmed: IR-002)

  Solar Panel Output → Battery Temperature
    Excessive solar exposure elevates both panel output and battery temperature
    simultaneously. High output (>215W) during attitude deviation is a
    thermal risk indicator. (Confirmed: IR-001)

  Battery Charge Level → CPU Temperature
    Low battery charge triggers power management mode which can cause
    voltage irregularities and CPU thermal events. (Confirmed: IR-006)

  Fuel Pressure → Velocity (delayed)
    Sustained fuel pressure below 200 kPa for >6 hours begins to impact
    thruster availability, which can cause gradual velocity drift.
    Impact is delayed 4-8 hours after pressure anomaly. (Confirmed: IR-003)

SECTION 3 — MISSION OPERATIONS BEST PRACTICES
================================================================================

3.1 ANOMALY INVESTIGATION FRAMEWORK
--------------------------------------
When an anomaly is detected, AETHER-1 operations follow this investigation order:

  Step 1 — Identify the primary anomalous parameter
  Step 2 — Check correlated parameters (Section 2.2 of OPS-MANUAL-001)
  Step 3 — Review trend data for the preceding 30-60 minutes
  Step 4 — Check recent maneuver history and scheduled task log
  Step 5 — Compare against historical pattern signatures (Section 1 above)
  Step 6 — Classify as known pattern or novel anomaly
  Step 7 — Apply standard response procedure or escalate for novel anomaly

3.2 SHIFT HANDOVER REQUIREMENTS
----------------------------------
Every shift handover must include:
  - Summary of all active WARNING and CRITICAL alerts
  - Current trend status for all monitored parameters
  - Scheduled maneuvers and tasks in next 8 hours
  - Any parameters approaching threshold (within 15% of WARNING level)
  - Open action items from previous shift

3.3 DOCUMENTATION REQUIREMENTS
---------------------------------
For every CRITICAL event:
  - Incident report must be filed within 24 hours
  - Root cause analysis must be completed within 72 hours
  - Lessons learned must be added to this knowledge base within 7 days

For every WARNING event:
  - Event log entry required within 4 hours
  - Trend monitoring report within 24 hours

SECTION 4 — ANOMALY CLASSIFICATION GUIDE
================================================================================

Use this guide to determine initial severity classification:

CRITICAL (Immediate Action Required):
  - Any parameter exceeding CRITICAL threshold (see Section 1, OPS-MANUAL-001)
  - Complete communication loss
  - Two or more WARNING parameters active simultaneously
  - Any parameter in uncontrolled deteriorating trend toward CRITICAL

WARNING (Prompt Investigation Required):
  - Any parameter in WARNING range
  - Any parameter showing consistent declining trend toward WARNING
  - Correlated parameter pair showing simultaneous early deviation
  - Maintenance schedule overdue by >10 days for critical procedures

INFO (Monitor and Log):
  - Parameter approaching WARNING threshold (within 10%)
  - Single isolated reading outside nominal but not in WARNING range
  - Scheduled maintenance reminder
