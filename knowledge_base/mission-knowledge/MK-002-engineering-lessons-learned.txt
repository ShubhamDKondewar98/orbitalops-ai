SECTION 1 — THERMAL MANAGEMENT LESSONS
================================================================================

LESSON TH-001: Early Thermal Intervention
------------------------------------------
Finding: In all AETHER-1 thermal events, temperatures that were allowed to
rise beyond 65°C required significantly longer recovery times (avg 2.5 hours)
compared to events caught at 55°C (avg 45 minutes).
Principle: Intervene at WARNING — do not wait for CRITICAL.
Application: Any thermal parameter crossing WARNING threshold should trigger
immediate load reduction, not just increased monitoring.

LESSON TH-002: Attitude-Thermal Correlation
---------------------------------------------
Finding: 78% of AETHER-1 battery temperature anomalies had attitude deviation
as a contributing or primary factor.
Principle: Thermal anomaly investigation must always include attitude check.
Application: When battery temperature exceeds 50°C, immediately assess
spacecraft attitude and solar exposure angle.

LESSON TH-003: CPU Thermal Throttling Impact
----------------------------------------------
Finding: CPU thermal throttling begins at 65°C and reduces clock speed
by up to 60% at 75°C. This directly impacts all time-critical processes
including attitude control loops.
Principle: CPU temperature is not isolated — it cascades to control systems.
Application: CPU temperature above 62°C should suspend all non-safety tasks
regardless of whether attitude deviation has been observed yet.

SECTION 2 — COMMUNICATION SYSTEM LESSONS
================================================================================

LESSON COM-001: Attitude is the Leading Communication Indicator
----------------------------------------------------------------
Finding: In every AETHER-1 communication degradation event, attitude
deviation was detectable before signal degradation began.
Principle: Monitor attitude stability as a predictive indicator for
communication health, not just as an independent parameter.
Application: Attitude deviation >±2.0 degrees should trigger communication
system health check immediately.

LESSON COM-002: Early Frequency Switching
-------------------------------------------
Finding: Teams that waited for complete signal loss before switching to
X-band backup experienced average 47-minute blackouts. Teams that switched
at -85dBm threshold experienced average 12-minute interruptions.
Principle: Failover early — the cost of unnecessary switching is minimal
compared to the cost of extended blackout.
Application: Establish automatic frequency switching at -85dBm. Do not
wait for complete loss.

LESSON COM-003: Blackout Duration Predictability
--------------------------------------------------
Finding: AETHER-1 blackout duration correlates with the degree of attitude
deviation at time of loss. Deviation of ±3-4 degrees: 30-60 min recovery.
Deviation >±4 degrees: >60 min recovery.
Principle: Attitude stability at time of signal loss predicts recovery time.
Application: Use attitude deviation data to set realistic recovery time
expectations and resource allocation for ground team response.

SECTION 3 — POWER SYSTEM LESSONS
================================================================================

LESSON PWR-001: Lagging vs Leading Indicators
-----------------------------------------------
Finding: Battery charge level is a lagging power health indicator. By the
time battery charge begins declining noticeably, the power deficit has
already been accumulating for hours.
Principle: Solar panel output trend is the leading power health indicator.
Application: Monitor 30-minute rolling average of solar panel output.
Declining trend below 200W requires immediate investigation regardless
of current battery charge level.

LESSON PWR-002: Load Shedding Timing
--------------------------------------
Finding: Power load shedding implemented when battery charge was above 70%
was highly effective (recovery rate: 94%). Load shedding at <50% charge
had lower effectiveness (recovery rate: 71%) due to compounding factors.
Principle: Implement load shedding early, before battery depletion begins.
Application: Trigger power load shedding at solar output WARNING threshold,
not at battery charge WARNING threshold.

LESSON PWR-003: Micrometeorite Impact Detection
-------------------------------------------------
Finding: Solar panel micrometeorite impacts do not cause sudden drops.
They cause gradual multi-day output decline. Single-point threshold
monitoring misses this entirely.
Principle: Panel health requires trend monitoring, not just threshold alerting.
Application: Implement 7-day rolling baseline comparison for solar output.
Alert on >5% decline from 7-day average.

SECTION 4 — PROPULSION SYSTEM LESSONS
================================================================================

LESSON PROP-001: Pressure Decline Rate Diagnosis
--------------------------------------------------
Finding: Gradual pressure decline (<15 kPa/hour) = supply restriction issue.
Rapid pressure drop (>30 kPa/hour) = valve or structural issue.
These have completely different response procedures.
Principle: Rate of change is diagnostic information, not just the value itself.
Application: Always calculate and log pressure decline rate alongside
absolute pressure value.

LESSON PROP-002: Filter Maintenance Compliance
------------------------------------------------
Finding: All filter occlusion events on AETHER-1 occurred when maintenance
interval exceeded 45 days. Zero occlusion events within 45-day interval.
Principle: Preventive maintenance compliance has perfect track record.
Application: Automated maintenance schedule reminders must be treated
as operational requirements, not suggestions.

SECTION 5 — ATTITUDE CONTROL LESSONS
================================================================================

LESSON ATT-001: Reaction Wheel Momentum Management
----------------------------------------------------
Finding: Reaction wheel saturation events are preventable with daily
desaturation. All saturation events on AETHER-1 occurred when automated
desaturation was interrupted by competing scheduled tasks.
Principle: Attitude control maintenance (desaturation) must have highest
task priority — never be deferred by other scheduled operations.
Application: Reaction wheel desaturation cannot be pre-empted by any
scheduled task. Implement as protected priority process.

LESSON ATT-002: Compound Attitude Events
------------------------------------------
Finding: Attitude deviation that simultaneously affects both communication
and thermal systems is significantly harder to recover from than isolated
attitude events. Average recovery time: 3.2 hours vs 45 minutes.
Principle: Multi-system impact from attitude deviation is a force multiplier
for mission disruption.
Application: Any attitude deviation must be assessed for secondary system
impacts within 5 minutes of detection.

SECTION 6 — MONITORING AND ALERTING LESSONS
================================================================================

LESSON MON-001: Correlated Parameter Monitoring Value
-------------------------------------------------------
Finding: In post-incident analysis, 82% of AETHER-1 CRITICAL events showed
early warning signals in correlated parameters 15-45 minutes before the
primary parameter reached CRITICAL threshold.
Principle: Correlated parameter monitoring provides earlier warning than
single-parameter threshold alerting.
Application: Every alert should automatically check and report status of
all correlated parameters (defined in OPS-MANUAL-001 Section 2.2).

LESSON MON-002: Trend Alerting vs Threshold Alerting
------------------------------------------------------
Finding: Threshold-only alerting missed gradual degradation events in
4 out of 5 cases reviewed. Trend-based alerting would have provided
15-60 minute earlier warning in each case.
Principle: Threshold alerting catches acute events. Trend alerting catches
chronic degradation. Both are needed.
Application: Implement both absolute threshold alerts AND rate-of-change
alerts for all monitored parameters.

LESSON MON-003: Alert Fatigue Prevention
------------------------------------------
Finding: During Mission Days 100-120, excessive low-severity alerts led
to reduced team responsiveness. Two WARNING events were acknowledged
late as a result.
Principle: Alert quality matters more than alert quantity.
Application: INFO alerts should be logged but not actively notified.
WARNING alerts trigger team notification. CRITICAL alerts trigger
immediate response protocol. Avoid alerting on transient single readings.
