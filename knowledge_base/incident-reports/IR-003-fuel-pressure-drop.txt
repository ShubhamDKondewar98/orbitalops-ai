INCIDENT SUMMARY
----------------
AETHER-1 propulsion system reported a gradual decline in fuel pressure over
a 3-hour monitoring window on Mission Day 113. Pressure dropped below the
WARNING threshold but did not reach CRITICAL levels. Root cause was identified
as a partially occluded fuel line filter. System was successfully restored
through a fuel line purge procedure.

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
--------------------
Primary Cause:
  Partial occlusion of the primary fuel line filter caused by micro-debris
  accumulation. The filter restriction reduced fuel flow rate, causing
  downstream pressure to drop gradually over several hours.

Contributing Factors:
  1. Filter maintenance purge cycle was last executed on Mission Day 60,
     exceeding the recommended 45-day interval.
  2. Micro-debris accumulation rate was higher than baseline predictions
     due to propellant quality variance noted during pre-launch fueling.
  3. Gradual pressure decline pattern made early detection difficult
     without trend analysis monitoring.

ACTIONS TAKEN
-------------
1. Executed fuel line purge procedure FP-003 to clear filter occlusion.
2. Increased fuel pressure monitoring frequency to 30-second intervals.
3. Logged filter maintenance overdue status — updated maintenance schedule.
4. Conducted full propulsion system health check post-recovery.

RESOLUTION
----------
Fuel pressure restored to nominal range within 1 hour of purge procedure.
No thruster performance degradation detected.
Propulsion system returned to full operational status.

LESSONS LEARNED
---------------
1. Filter maintenance purge cycles must not exceed 45-day intervals —
   automated maintenance scheduling alert added to mission calendar.
2. Gradual declining trends require trend-based alerting, not just
   threshold-based alerting — monitoring logic updated.
3. Propellant quality variance should trigger more frequent filter
   inspection schedules for future missions.

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FUTURE MISSIONS
-------------------------------------
1. Implement trend-based anomaly detection for slowly declining parameters.
2. Add automated filter purge reminder at 40-day intervals.
3. Conduct propellant quality analysis before finalizing maintenance schedules.
4. Add fuel pressure rate-of-change monitoring alongside absolute thresholds.
