INCIDENT SUMMARY
----------------
On Mission Day 47, AETHER-1 onboard telemetry reported a rapid increase in
battery temperature exceeding the nominal operating threshold. The anomaly
triggered automated safe mode entry and required immediate ground intervention.

TELEMETRY AT TIME OF INCIDENT
------------------------------

------------------------|-------------------|-------------------|--------
Battery Temperature     | 10°C – 45°C       | 82°C              | CRITICAL
Battery Charge Level    | 60% – 95%         | 91%               | NORMAL
Solar Panel Output      | 180W – 220W       | 214W              | NORMAL
Onboard CPU Temperature | 20°C – 60°C       | 58°C              | WARNING
Signal Strength         | -90dBm – -60dBm   | -72dBm            | NORMAL
Altitude                | 520km – 540km     | 531km             | NORMAL

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
--------------------
Primary Cause:
  Thermal regulation instability caused by prolonged direct solar exposure
  during an unplanned orbital orientation shift. The spacecraft attitude
  control system failed to execute a scheduled rotation maneuver, leaving
  solar-facing battery panels exposed for an extended duration.

Contributing Factors:
  1. Attitude Stability deviation of 4.2 degrees from nominal caused incorrect
     solar angle alignment.
  2. Thermal Management subsystem did not trigger cooling cycle due to a
     software flag conflict with safe mode logic.
  3. High CPU temperature (58°C) indicated elevated onboard processing load
     which contributed to internal heat generation.

ACTIONS TAKEN
-------------
1. Reduced non-essential power load by 35% to lower thermal output.
2. Manually triggered attitude correction maneuver from ground control.
3. Overrode safe mode flag to allow thermal management cooling cycle.
4. Increased battery temperature monitoring frequency from 60s to 10s intervals.
5. Initiated full thermal subsystem diagnostic post-recovery.

RESOLUTION
----------
Battery temperature normalized within 2.5 hours of initial detection.
No permanent damage to battery cells detected.
Attitude correction maneuver successfully executed.
Thermal management system restored to automatic operation.

LESSONS LEARNED
---------------
1. Attitude stability deviations greater than 2 degrees should trigger
   immediate thermal risk assessment — threshold updated in monitoring system.
2. Safe mode flag logic must not suppress thermal management cooling cycles
   under any condition — software patch deployed (Patch ID: SW-047-THERMAL).
3. Ground team response time can be reduced by pre-defining thermal emergency
   runbooks accessible directly from mission control dashboard.

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FUTURE MISSIONS
-------------------------------------
1. Implement redundant thermal sensors on battery panels.
2. Add automated attitude-thermal correlation check in onboard flight software.
3. Set battery temperature WARNING threshold to 50°C (reduced from 55°C).
4. Conduct quarterly thermal stress simulation tests.
