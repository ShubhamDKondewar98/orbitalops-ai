INCIDENT SUMMARY
----------------
AETHER-1 solar panel output showed a gradual degradation trend over Mission
Days 120-133, declining from a baseline of 210W to 162W — a 23% reduction.
Investigation identified partial shadowing due to micrometeorite impact debris
on Panel Array B, combined with normal solar cell degradation accelerated by
cumulative radiation exposure.

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
--------------------
Primary Cause:
  Micrometeorite impact on Panel Array B created surface debris and minor
  structural damage to approximately 18% of Array B photovoltaic cells.
  Debris shadowing reduced effective light capture area significantly.

Contributing Factors:
  1. Cumulative radiation exposure over 133 mission days caused baseline
     solar cell efficiency degradation of approximately 4-6% beyond
     pre-launch projections.
  2. Current mission orbital altitude (534km) passes through elevated
     micrometeorite flux region identified in post-launch orbital analysis.
  3. Battery charge level declining in parallel indicated the power deficit
     was not being compensated by battery reserves alone.

ACTIONS TAKEN
-------------
1. Implemented power load shedding — reduced non-critical system consumption by 28W.
2. Adjusted spacecraft attitude by 7 degrees to maximize Panel Array A exposure.
3. Reduced battery deep discharge cycles to protect remaining battery health.
4. Scheduled orbital altitude adjustment to exit elevated flux region.
5. Updated power budget model with new degraded baseline values.

RESOLUTION
----------
Status: MONITORING — full resolution pending orbital altitude adjustment.
Interim measures have stabilized power budget within acceptable margins.
Panel Array B permanent repair not possible via remote operation.
Mission continues with adjusted power budget and load management plan.

LESSONS LEARNED
---------------
1. Solar panel output trend monitoring over 7-day windows should be
   standard — single-point threshold alerts missed gradual degradation.
2. Orbital altitude selection must weight micrometeorite flux data more
   heavily in pre-mission planning phases.
3. Battery charge level and solar output should be monitored as correlated
   pair — combined decline is a stronger signal than either alone.

RECOMMENDATIONS FOR FUTURE MISSIONS
-------------------------------------
1. Implement 7-day rolling average trend alerts for solar panel output.
2. Add micrometeorite flux risk zones to orbital planning constraints.
3. Design panel arrays with debris-resistant surface coatings.
4. Implement correlated parameter monitoring for power system health.
5. Maintain 15% power budget margin for degradation contingency.
